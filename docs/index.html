Before class

Three things. About ten minutes. You do not download any class files, we do that together in the first few minutes.

  1. Sign in to an AI tool. Claude Code is smoothest. GitHub Copilot is free for students with your DU email. ChatGPT Plus or Gemini also work. Details in Which tool you need.
  2. Bring one real decision from work. Something your team has to settle in the next month or two, where two or more options are genuinely on the table.
  3. Do not bring company data you are unsure about. Read Your company's data below. There is practice data for everything.

What counts as a good decision to bring

The test is whether someone could choose differently after seeing your analysis.

  • Should we hire two people or extend the contractor arrangement?
  • Should we raise prices on this product line, and by how much?
  • Which of these three problems do we fix first?
  • Is this vendor worth renewing?

Session 1 and 2

The five stages

Each stage feeds the next. Session 1 is stages 1 and 2, which is why we do not build anything the first night. Session 2 is stages 3 to 5.

  1. 1

    Frame the decision

    Turn a vague ask into a question you can answer. Who owns the decision, what forces it, and what evidence would actually change their mind. This is the stage people want to skip and the one that decides whether the rest is useful.

    Say to your AIhelp me frame my decision
  2. 2

    Audit the data

    Find out whether your data can carry the weight you are about to put on it. Missing values, duplicates, outliers, and columns that quietly changed meaning three years ago. Every change gets logged with a reason.

    Say to your AIaudit my data before I analyze it
  3. 3

    Build your use case

    Pick one of the ten below and build it. You arrive at session 2 with a framed decision and audited data, which is what makes an hour enough time.

    Say to your AIwhich skill should I use?
  4. 4

    Write the decision memo

    One page, recommendation first. Your reader spends ninety seconds on it. If they read only your first paragraph and acted, they should act correctly.

    Say to your AIwrite this up for my VP
  5. 5

    Demo it

    Five minutes. Open with the decision, then the answer, then show two or three things, then say honestly what it cannot do. That last part earns the most credibility in the room.

    Say to your AIhelp me prep my demo, then play the skeptical VP

Your company's data

You do not need to bring any data from work.

You all have obligations to your employers and those come first. If you are unsure whether something can leave your company's systems, the answer is that it stays there.

The workspace includes practice datasets built for every exercise. They are realistic, messy on purpose, and completely fictional.

  • You can build the whole thing on practice data and point it at your real numbers later, inside your own systems, on your own time. Several of you will find that is the right call and you will not be behind.
  • If you do use work data, check your employer's policy on AI tools first. Most organizations have one and most people have not read it.
  • Personal identifiers do not belong in the workspace. Names, emails, employee or customer numbers, salary or health details. Aggregate or remove them first.
  • Anything you put in the sources/raw folder stays on your own machine and is never shared.

Why a free chat tool cannot do the number-heavy ones

AI writes fluent, confident prose about arithmetic and gets it wrong in ways that are very hard to spot. Tools that actually run code produce a real calculation you can check. If a tool cannot run code, do not trust it with a number you are going to present. That is one of the lessons of the session, not a limitation of the class.

Troubleshooting

If you get stuck

Your work laptop blocks the installation

Common and not a problem. Use the browser version of whichever tool you picked and come to class. Nothing we do requires administrator rights.

Your company network blocks the browser version too

Come anyway, and come to session 1 especially. Session 1 is framing your decision and interrogating your data, which is thinking work. There is a paper template for every stage, and the next stage picks up from your filled-in template exactly as if you had done it with AI.

Three things that work without touching your work laptop:

  • Your own phone. A personal phone on cellular is not on your company network. The decision-framing conversation works fine in a phone browser, and that is the single most valuable part of session 1.
  • Pair with someone. You bring the decision and the domain knowledge, they drive the tool. This is genuinely how it works at work.
  • Paper, then catch up later. Fill the templates in class, run them through a tool on a personal machine afterward. Nothing is lost.

Do not try to get around your employer's network restrictions. They exist for a reason and none of this is worth it. If you want the tools on that machine, the conversation to have is with your IT team, not with the VPN.

Something will not install

None of it is required. Without Node you copy one file by hand. Without Python you lose the file converter, and the practice data is already included. Without Git you download a zip file instead.

The AI cannot find the workspace files

Tell it where to look: read AGENTS.md and follow it. If the skills are not picked up automatically, say read .agents/skills/find-skills/SKILL.md and follow it.

You put the folder in the wrong place

Move it to your user folder, not Documents or Desktop. Folders synced to OneDrive can corrupt the hidden files a code project needs, and long folder paths break on Windows.

You are behind

Every stage has a paper template you can fill in by hand, and the next stage picks up from it exactly as if you had done it with AI. Falling back to paper is a supported path, not a failure.

Plain definitions

Words we will use

You will hear these in class. None of them require a statistics background.

Confidence interval
The range of values consistent with your data. Its width tells you how much you actually learned. A narrow range means you know something, a wide one means the honest answer is "we are not sure yet."
Statistical significance
Whether a difference is bigger than what random chance would plausibly produce. It does not tell you whether the difference is big enough to be worth acting on. Those are two separate questions and both need answering.
p-value
If there were truly no difference, how often would you see a gap at least this large? A p-value of 0.03 means about 3 times in 100. It is not the chance that you are wrong.
Baseline
The simple guess your fancy method has to beat. Often "the same as last year." If the fancy method does not beat it, use the simple one and save yourself the maintenance.
Target leakage
When a column secretly contains the answer, usually because it gets filled in after the thing you are predicting already happened. It makes a model look brilliant in testing and useless in real life.
Correlation versus cause
Two things moving together does not mean one causes the other. A model can tell you which customers to call. It cannot tell you that changing the thing it flagged will change the outcome.
BLUF
Bottom Line Up Front. Put the recommendation in the first paragraph, before the background and the method. It is how busy people read.
Cleaning log
A written record of every change you made to the data and why. It is what you pull up when someone challenges a number three weeks later.
