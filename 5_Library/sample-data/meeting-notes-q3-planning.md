# Q3 Planning — Operations Review

*Fictional. Built for the `meeting-to-actions` skill. Messy on purpose: some decisions are
buried mid-sentence, two "owners" are ambiguous, one deadline conflicts with another, and at
least one thing everybody treated as decided was never actually decided.*

**Attendees:** Priya (VP Operations), Marcus (Support Director), Dana (Finance), Tom (Product),
Aisha (Implementation Lead), Ken (IT), Rachel (HR Business Partner)

---

Priya opened by saying we're 11 days into the quarter and the ticket backlog is the thing keeping
her up at night. 2,340 open tickets, up from about 1,600 at the end of Q2. Marcus said the backlog
number is misleading because roughly 400 of those are auto-generated from the monitoring
integration and nobody has triaged whether they're real. Priya asked him to get a clean number.
Marcus said he could have it by the end of next week, Dana pushed back and said she needs it for
the board deck which goes to print on the 24th, so Marcus said fine, the 22nd.

Discussion on hiring. Rachel walked through the approved req list — four support reps and one team
lead, all approved in the Q2 cycle, none posted yet. Rachel said the delay is on her side, the job
descriptions have been sitting in draft. Marcus said honestly the bigger problem is that even if we
hire today, ramp is eight weeks and we won't feel it until October. Dana asked what overtime would
cost as a bridge. Nobody had the number. Dana said she'd model it. Priya said let's look at both —
overtime bridge versus contractor bridge — before we commit to either.

Tom raised the self-service deflection work. The help center rewrite is about 60% done. He thinks
if it shipped it would take maybe 8-12% of ticket volume out, mostly password and billing
questions. Marcus was skeptical — said we've heard the deflection number before and it never lands
where it's predicted. Tom agreed it's an estimate. Priya said we should ship it anyway because the
downside is small, and asked Tom when. Tom said end of August if he doesn't lose his writer to the
onboarding project. Aisha said she does need that writer for onboarding docs in August. That did
not get resolved.

Ken brought up the SSO migration. He needs a maintenance window and the only clean one is a
Saturday. He proposed August 8th. Dana said that's month-end close weekend for Finance and asked
for a different date. Ken said the following Saturday works, August 15th. Everyone seemed fine with
that. Ken said he'd send the calendar invite and notify the customer base two weeks out, so by
August 1st.

Aisha reported on implementation. Average time to first value is now 34 days, down from 47 in Q1,
which she attributed to the new kickoff checklist. Priya said that's the best number in the deck
and asked her to write up what changed so other teams can copy it. No date was set on that.

On the reporting request from last month — Marcus said his team still can't schedule a recurring
export to leadership, which means someone manually rebuilds the weekly ops summary every Monday.
Tom said it's on the roadmap for Q4. Marcus said Q4 is four months of somebody's Monday morning.
Priya asked Tom to look at whether there's a manual workaround in the meantime. Tom said he'd check
with the platform team.

Dana closed with budget. We are 6% under on headcount spend because of the unfilled reqs, and 14%
over on contractor spend, which roughly cancels out. She flagged that if the reqs fill in August
the run rate changes materially and she'd want to revisit the Q4 forecast in September.

Priya's last comment: "We are not going to fix the backlog with any one of these. We need the
clean number first, and then we pick two of the four levers." Meeting ended over time; the vendor
consolidation item was pushed to next week.
